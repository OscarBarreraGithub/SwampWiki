# Run an atlas on a new paper collection

Start with a folder of PDFs and a short description of its intended scope. The workflow produces a source index, a versioned question graph with embeddings, and a report showing whether people and agents can find the right questions and evidence. Geophysics is the next application. The batch sizes below are pilot defaults.

1. **Freeze the input and pilot the paper record.** Inventory PDFs by DOI or other stable ID, record version and content hash, and log duplicates, unreadable files and missing metadata. Sample roughly 50 papers across topics, dates and methods. For each, Claude extracts distinct research questions, results, methods, assumptions, conventions, regimes and limitations. Give each item a source locator such as a page, figure, table or equation. Preserve author claims and reviewer findings as separate records. For geophysics, capture the system or location studied, observed versus modeled quantities, spatial and temporal scales, units, data or instruments, and uncertainty and inversion assumptions where applicable. An independent reader checks a sample against the PDFs before extraction is scaled.
2. **Build geometry from the records.** Embed research questions and methods as separate views. Compare term-based, graph-only and embedding-assisted candidates on the same inputs. Turn promising groups into scoped questions a researcher can read, with examples at the center and at the boundary. Use stable destination IDs. Preserve different hypotheses and regimes as distinct linked destinations. Use affinity to nominate links and attach source evidence before promoting a scientific relation. Keep narrower topics, related scientific questions and paper evidence legible as different choices. Show as few as the page needs, usually a handful and up to roughly 15, with the complete connections accessible.
3. **Test the routes before scaling.** Set aside 15–25 real researcher questions, with expected targets or documented gaps chosen from the sources independently of the builder. Check whether browsing and an agent given plain prose converge on the same scoped destination, whether a changed premise gives a different result, and whether a lateral route and a shared link recover the right evidence and qualifications. Test the actual first screen and any links a ranking change displaces. Use a smaller development set for repairs and freeze the candidate before scoring held-out cases. Record coverage, specific matches, honest gaps, and navigation failures separately. Expert feedback can supply realistic research questions, correct scientific errors, and improve the field's framing.
4. **Scale and verify.** Run resumable batches with one output per paper and a coverage ledger. Reserve deep source checks for disagreements, high-impact claims and structurally important edges. Give every audit finding an evidence link, an owner and an executable next task. Give implementers scoped access to the files they need. Check the served build independently of fixtures, including slow-network source access and replay of old links. Freeze and publish the candidate only after its acceptance checks pass.

SwampWiki's [independent review](SW-005-independent-atlas-review.md) documents the main failure this workflow targets. In a catalog where almost every broad destination could be reached through some route, very few could be reached by topical narrowing alone, and paper examples accounted for 42% of choices on authored question pages. A transferable test needs to measure what the reader sees at each step as well as what the graph contains.
